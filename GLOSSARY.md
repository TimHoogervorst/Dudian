# Chinese reading practice

The learner reads Chinese stories and uses encounters with unfamiliar language to guide future practice.

## Language

**Story catalog**

The collection of stories available to all learners. Each story has a common text and character focus, and each learner has their own reading history with it.

**Character focus**

The characters a story is designed to practice. Every learner sees the same focus characters alongside their own current status for each one.

**Strong focus**

The highest story focus tier, covering the words and characters selected for deliberate practice. A character can be a strong focus target at any story difficulty and at any learner reading strength.

**Reading type**

The form of reading the learner chooses for a practice session. Book uses narrative prose, Article presents material such as notices, letters, recipes, and reports, and Dialogue presents a conversation as messages.

**Reading subtype**

A more specific form within a reading type, such as a children’s book or a recipe. The learner’s HSK progress guides which subtypes can appear.

**Character**  
A single written Chinese symbol, such as 东. Its Library entry links to words that contain it.

**Introduction**

The opening page of a reading session. It presents the cover illustration, teaser, chapters, and vocabulary preview before the learner starts reading.

**Word**  
A vocabulary entry made up of one or more characters, such as 东西. A word can be looked up in a story and practiced as a whole.

**Cast member**

A recurring figure in Dudian’s story world. The steamed-bun protagonist is a cast member from the learner’s first story.

**包包 (Bāobāo)**

Dudian’s recurring steamed-bun protagonist, recognizable by a cream-colored pleated top, rosy cheeks, tiny limbs, and a green crossbody satchel.

**Reading strength**  
An estimate of how reliably the learner recognizes and understands an item while reading.

**Background vocabulary**  
Words available to build a story around its learning goals. The learner starts with the HSK 1 word pool available for this purpose.

**Incidental appearance**

A character or word the learner encounters while reading a chapter outside its focus and without a question testing that item.

**Overdue review**

A recall check due after a gap in practice. The item keeps its reading strength while it waits for review.

**Last seen**

The time the learner most recently encountered a particular character or word. Seeing an item again while its review is overdue leaves the recall check pending.

**Perfect**

The endpoint of Strong for an individual character or word. A Perfect item retains its reading strength through time away and mistakes in practice. The learner can still lower it manually.

**Review target**  
A previously encountered character or word selected for deliberate practice in a story.

**Lookup**  
A request to see a word’s meaning in its sentence. A learner can use a lookup to check their own translation.

**Difficulty mark**  
The learner’s explicit judgment that they struggled with a particular word in context.

**Story rating**  
The learner’s thumbs-up or thumbs-down judgment of a story.

**Comprehension check**

A short set of multiple-choice questions the learner answers at the end of a chapter. Each question tests vocabulary through the chapter’s events, messages, or information.

**Flashcard review**  
A practice attempt in which the learner recalls the pronunciation and meaning of the displayed Chinese, reveals the answer, and assesses their recall.

**Library practice exercise**

A short task that tests the learner on one or more characters or words. Choosing the English meaning of 水 is one exercise, as is matching five Chinese entries to their English meanings.

**Practice target**

A character or word whose reading strength is assessed in a Library practice exercise. Each pair in a matching exercise has its own practice target.

**Matching exercise**

A Library practice exercise in which the learner pairs Chinese entries with their English meanings and submits the whole set with one check. Each Chinese entry receives its own result based on the meaning paired with it at submission.

**Milestone test**

A generated assessment available once a learner has reached a whole HSK level. Passing it allows the progress bar to advance beyond that level.

**Test section**

One part of a milestone test. A reading section contains a Book passage, Article, or Dialogue with a comprehension check. A word-practice section contains vocabulary exercises.

**Practice retake**

A fresh milestone test taken for practice at an HSK level the learner has already passed. Its result leaves the earned pass, HSK progress, and reading strength unchanged.
